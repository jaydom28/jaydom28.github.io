## Starting My Own JustinKasten

I used to be really nerdy about personal knowledge management systems. I spent so much time researching what the best way to implement zettelkastens, digital gardens, second brains and personal wikis. For those who don't know, personal knowledge management is just a way to organize relevant information and notes you take. A good note taking app will offer organization beyond just folders through things like tags and making links between notes.

Some people have crazy systems where they use something like [Notion](https://www.notion.com/) and things are all automated and handled with LLMs. For me, that is a bit overkill and I try to just find a system that works for me and doesn't have too much friction because I think the best system is just one you will actually use. So I am trademarking my own system called, the JustinKasten!

## What Is A JustinKasten?

The JustinKasten takes inspiration from all the other PKMS. I have two main folders:

1. JustinKasten (things I wrote myself)
2. Second Brain (interesting things other people wrote that I webclip then highlight/annotate)

Then of course there are links between all the notes in these two folders. That's it, that's the JustinKasten!

## Why Have a JustinKasten?

One might ask why even have a PKMS if we have search engines. I am [not against AI]({{< relref "/posts/Changing-Perspective-on-AI/" >}}), but **the mainstream integration of LLM generated results in search engines is the worst thing to happen to friend group debates.** The average person won't fact check. Everyone's time is too precious, we need answers and we needed them yesterday!

A while back I had an amicable discussion with a friend about my Chinese zodiac being different from my Khmer zodiac. My friend was skeptical and decided to google it, which yielded an LLM generated response.

![](https://cdn.britannica.com/80/267080-050-D2A05320/chinese-zodiac.jpg)

Now before we move on, there are some nuances about how these zodiac systems work. If you look up the Chinese zodiac, you're likely to find charts and other things saying the entire year correlates to an animal. This is misleading, the true cutoff date for the zodiac is **lunar new year** and not the standard new year, so **not** January 1st. Looking at the chart above, someone who doesn't know this real cutoff will think that a person born on January 1st, 1998 is a tiger. That is wrong, they are an Ox because lunar new year typically falls later in the month around January 28th.

So this is where it gets more interesting. In the Khmer zodiac, the cutoff date falls on **Khmer New Year**, typically on April 13th. So taking into account these two distinct cutoff dates, someone born on, let's say, January 28, 1998 would be a **tiger** in the Chinese zodiac and an **ox** in the Khmer zodiac. I understand that Wikipedia isn't the best thing to reference, but the Khmer zodiac cutoff dates can be found [here](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambodian_New_Year) and [here](https://humanoriginproject.com/the-khmer-calendar-cambodian-religion-festivals-and-zodiac-astrology/).

Long story short, my friend showed me the LLM generated response which said that I am the same animal in both the Chinese and Khmer zodiacs. Then he told me that my traditional Cambodian family is wrong. Moments like this should be acknowledged, because it shows how little effort people are willing to put in to thoroughly search for information, I am guilty of this and do it all the time. Anyway, fact checking is important, peace out.
