I have some big news (or at least big news for me) which is that I am writing a small Khmer crash course. My original idea was for it to be just a series of small chapters but I think a good long-term goal would be for it to slowly become a dedicated grammar wiki.

## Why A Khmer Grammar Wiki?

For context, I am a Khmer heritage speaker. What this means is, I grew up around Khmer all the time but, being an American, my ability to speak it is not the greatest. In the majority of my daily life I do not need to speak Khmer. One thing that has stuck out to me, is that it's pretty hard to find resources to learn Khmer. I study a good deal of Mandarin, and I treat [this public Mandarin grammar wiki](https://resources.allsetlearning.com/chinese/grammar/Main_Page) as a kind of bible. There is also [this Japanese grammar wiki](https://en.wikibooks.org/wiki/Japanese). I haven't found anything like this for Khmer and so I thought to myself "why not start it"? 

## What Will It Be?
The thing about a task like this is, there's really no end. By starting this, I am basically signing up to be the forever maintainer. My vision for the beginning is just for it to be a small wiki to explain grammar topics. People will be able to give feedback and suggestions for the individual pages, but ultimately all the changes will have to be made by me. Each page will be focused on a single grammar topic. For this, I do not really have a concrete plan in mind. What will happen is, when I am doing my Khmer comprehensible input (either reading articles or watching Youtube videos), I will just make a note of any interesting grammar that shows up. Then I will decide from there if it should be its own grammar wiki page. It will grow organically and I expect it will stay be pretty small for a while. If it grows to be something bigger, then I will figure out what to do then along the lines of separating it from my website and myself, and allowing others to contribute directly.

## Some Challenges

There are two big challenges I'm facing when it comes to this:
1. I suck at writing and explaining.
2. It is hard to get help on this.

I have spent a great deal of time studying all kinds of languages, so I am used to figuring out and understanding grammar. However, writing these pages forced me to actually explain grammar that I thought I knew. The process makes me wonder if I even knew it in the first place. Writing is not easy for me. It's funny to me how you think you know something until you're asked to either explain it or write it out.

The second challenge is interesting. One thing that makes all language tricky is, everybody has their own way of saying things which they think is the right and natural way. Also it is tricky sifting through help and feedback, while being mindful of when and where to push back just a bit. For example, my dad has been a source of help and I asked him how to say "I quit my job" in Khmer. The way I would say it is

<span class="pronoun">ខ្ញុំ</span> <span class="verb">លាឈប់</span> <span class="direct-obj">ការងារ</span>
: <span class="pronoun">kyom</span> <span class="verb">lie chup</span> <span class="direct-obj">ka-gnear</span>
: <span class="pronoun">I</span> <span class="verb">quit</span> <span class="direct-obj">my job.</span>

However, he told me that he would just say

<span class="pronoun">ខ្ញុំ</span> <span class="verb">ឈប់</span> <span class="direct-obj">ធ្វើការ</span>
: <span class="pronoun">kyom</span> <span class="verb">chup</span> <span class="direct-obj">tveur-kaa</span>
: <span class="pronoun">I</span> <span class="verb">stopped</span> <span class="direct-obj">working.</span>

and reassured me that most Khmer people would understand this sentence to mean quitting, given the right context. But the problem is, the meaning in what I am trying to say is fundamentally changed. I don't want to say "I stopped working" and hope that given the context it will mean what I actually want to say. But now there's the question of "what's technically right?" If I think it's wrong to use the second sentence, but most people would use it, does that mean I am wrong? Is it weird and unnatural for me to use the actual word?

Another tricky area is what I think a lot of heritage speakers go through. It can be really discouraging depending on who you're talking to and asking for help from. So my dad has made content for teaching Khmer in the past. He has a teacher's mind and he's used to my dumb questions. I have asked for help on certain sentences or word choice from my mom however, and she will have no idea what I am trying to say. My conversations with my mom will go like this

> Hey ma, how do you say XYZ in Khmer? Would it be ABC?

> What the hell are you trying to say?

So you can see how if I wasn't already objectively the best, handsomest, and smartest linguist in the world, I would get discouraged. Then I'll go and ask my dad the same exact question and he magically knows exactly what I am saying and trying to ask and can give me an answer. So I guess the takeaway here is to be mindful of who you're asking. Because if you're only talking to "what the hell are you trying to say" people, then you're gonna get discouraged. Luckily I personally know a lot of people who speak Khmer as a first language, meaning I can get more than just my dad's help.

But anyway I thought this would be a fun project and would help a lot of people I know personally. If I am fortunate enough, it will help even more people.
